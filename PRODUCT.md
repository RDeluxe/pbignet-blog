# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Peers, fellow engineers and creatives, and occasional new readers who discover the site through social or search. They arrive with curiosity about the person behind the work — not needing a sales pitch, but wanting proof of taste and depth. Secondary: future collaborators or employers scanning the index as a portfolio signal.

## Product Purpose

A personal index for Pierrick Bignet — engineer, creative director, photographer. Not a typical blog: a living archive of thinking, photographs, and gear, presented as a printed artifact. The site is the work as much as the writing is. Success means a visitor understands who Pierrick is within 10 seconds and wants to read further.

## Brand Personality

Analogue precision. Print-native. Quietly obsessive.

The tone is specific and confident, never declarative or promotional. The design speaks through materials (paper, grain, dither, topo) not through copy about itself.

## Anti-references

- Generic cream/beige Framer portfolio templates (warm background without material texture)
- SaaS landing pages with stat counters and gradient text
- "Brutalist" blogs that are stark for its own sake, without print warmth
- Editorial sites that are purely typographic with no photographic material

## Design Principles

1. **The medium is the message.** The dithering, grain, and topographic texture aren't decorative — they explain how Pierrick sees things. Every visual choice should carry information about the person.
2. **Print furniture earns its place.** Registration marks, barcodes, coordinates, index numbers: each one should be there because a printed artifact would have it, not because it looks cool. Decoration masquerading as data is the failure mode.
3. **Photos are primary.** This is a photographer's site. The dithering pipeline exists to give photographs their own visual grammar; they shouldn't look like stock images or placeholders.
4. **Committed decisions, not safe ones.** The hero is big and specific. The locked direction (RETRO v2) is a committed choice — the photo literally lives inside the type. Don't soften it.
5. **The body earns the hero.** The below-fold content (writing list, field guides, photos, setup) must carry the same design personality as the hero. The visitor should feel the same intent in the smallest component as in the full-page opening.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Focus rings are already implemented in paper.css (`a:focus-visible` rule). Reduced motion handling needed for the dither canvas and hero photo-alignment script. Color should not be the sole carrier of meaning (accent blue for emphasis is supplementary to text cues).
